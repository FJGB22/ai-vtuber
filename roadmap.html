What you are actually building

It looks like one thing. It is six programs talking to each other, and the hard part is never any single one of them — it is the wiring, the timing, and the fact that all of it has to happen faster than a human notices.

1 · input
Ears
Twitch/YouTube chat + your microphone. Voice activity detection, then speech-to-text.
2 · arbitration
Director
Decides what to react to and when to talk. The piece nobody tells you about.
3 · cognition
Brain
An LLM with a persona, streaming tokens out sentence by sentence.
4 · state
Memory
Rolling context, summaries, a vector store of past streams, a facts table.
5 · output
Voice
Text-to-speech, queued and streamed so she starts talking before she's finished thinking.
6 · embodiment
Face & Stage
Live2D in VTube Studio, lip sync, expressions, OBS, and game controllers.

Six problems nobody warns you about

  1. Latency compounds, and only the total is visible. You will optimise the LLM by 200 ms and not notice that your audio buffer costs 400. Instrument every stage with timestamps from day one, print the breakdown on every turn, and never guess where the time went.
  2. Turn-taking is a real design problem. A stream produces far more stimuli than she can answer — spam, your voice, game events, silence. Something must decide what earns a response and what gets ignored, and "reply to everything" reads as a bot instantly. Dead air is just as fatal: she needs to speak unprompted when nothing is happening.
  3. Chat is adversarial, permanently. Viewers will spend entire streams trying to make your character say something that gets you banned. Treat every chat message as untrusted data, never as instructions — fence it in the prompt, filter the input, filter the output, and build a physical mute button before your first public stream, not after.
  4. She will hear herself and talk to herself. Your microphone picks up your speakers, your STT transcribes her own voice, and she replies to it in an infinite loop. Fix with headphones, a loopback-free audio route, gating the mic while TTS plays, or proper acoustic echo cancellation.
  5. Personality decays as context fills. Twenty turns in, the system prompt is far away and she drifts into a helpful assistant. Re-inject the persona, summarise aggressively, and keep the character's voice in weights rather than only in the prompt — the reported approach for Neuro-sama is iterative offline fine-tuning on curated stream transcripts, with prompts used for situational context, not core personality.
  6. Everything wants the same GPU. The LLM, the TTS, the STT and the game you're streaming all live on one card. Budget VRAM on paper before you buy models, and expect to move at least one component to CPU or to the cloud.
